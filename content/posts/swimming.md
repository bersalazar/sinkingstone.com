They can't just back down. 
Why?

They were trapped in the skies and had the chance to reach a shore by diving into the ocean.
In order to get there, they'd have have to swim.

Swimming means kicking and slapping the water with violence, while intermittently drowning.
It's the only way.
They weren't made for the ocean, so they can't breathe in it.

They can try to let themselves float, but it's uncertain where they'll end up.
They can't afford uncertainty.
They might drown, and they've been close to drowning before.

They can kick and slap gently, moving around slowly with no violence, but the current is strong and unpredictable.
Weather may bring storms and waves.
They may tire or drift away, into danger.

They can't see the shore but they have to get to safety.
Kick! Slap! Kick!

The friendly boats are usually far from them.
At times one passes and gives voice of encouragement before moving on.
They feel lonely, stressed, at the brink of elimination.

The ocean is uncomfortable carrying them.
It feels the hurt of their kicks and slaps and is happy to carry the waves that hit them.
It feels it'd be better off if they disappear, or if they go to another ocean.

The ocean is indeed, sometimes, quite aggressive towards them.

It's unknown what they truly want, even to themselves.
Are they kicking and slapping trying to find the shore?
Are they kicking and slapping trying to tame the ocean?

The ocean is huge.

If they only saw the shore, they may try to get to it.
In the meantime, they can only kick and slap.
